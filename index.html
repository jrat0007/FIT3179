The immediate impact of COVID-19 on the aviation industry

CO₂ emissions of passenger air travel from OECD countries

The OECD (Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development) is an international organisation consisting of 38 member countries with the goal of working with "policy makers, stakeholders and citizens to establish evidence-based international standards and to find solutions to social, economic and environmental challenges" (https://www.oecd.org/en/about.html).

CO₂ Emissions — OECD Countries (excluding US)

CO₂ Emissions — United States

This is the CO₂ emissions as reported by members of the OECD. The United States is presented on a different graph as the scale of CO₂ emissions from the United States is on a much different scale to that of the other countries. We see that in all of these member countries, the emissions drop drastically after 2020, corresponding to a large deficit in passenger travel after the COVID pandemic hit. It can be said that in combination with efficiency improvements, the CO₂ emissions has returned to a non-COVID-19 state.

Energy usage in the aviation industry in Australia

As we can see, energy usage in the aviation industy was steadily increasing in Australia until the financial year of 2019-20. This could imply that the aviation industry was growing. As we see there is a sharp drop from the financial year of 2018-19 to the financial year of 2020-21, this is likely due to the COVID-19 pandemic. We can see that by the financial year of 2023-2024, the Australian aviation industry has mostly recovered.

Australian airport passenger traffic

As we can see from this proportional symbol map, the major airports got affected a lot more than the regional airports by the COVID pandemic. This would make sense as non-passenger aviation isn't affected as much by pandemic and epidemics as passenger aviation. It should be noted that the data for 2025 is up to June and as such is not complete. Comparing the 2024 data to pre-COVID-19 data, we can see that airport passenger traffic has mostly returned to its regular state.

Australian Passenger Aircraft Movements

Here we see that the domestic and regional aircraft movements has always been much greater than the international aircraft movements. Both see a drop in 2020, but domestic and regional aircraft movement sees a much larger drop based on magnitude. However, international aircraft movements sees a larger proportional reduction than domestic and regional aircraft movements. It should be noted that the data for 2025 is up to June and as such is not complete. Comparing the 2024 data to pre-COVID-19 data, we can see that passenger aircraft movements in Australia has returned to normal for international flights. For domestic and regional flights, it is still somewhat recovering, however it is almost fully recovered.

Overseas arrivals and departures by state

We see that overseas arrivals and departures for each of the Australian states follow a very similar trend, with a sharp drop in 2020 where the COVID-19 pandemic started having its impact on the aviation industry. We also see that most of the states have very similar arrival and departure numbers, with the exception of NSW and TAS.
